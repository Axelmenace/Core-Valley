# Operative Mode Framework, Draft 0.4: §25 Magic System Construction

**Contains:** the Author Hardness Principle, the three Tiers of a magic system, Tendency Rules and Discretion Points, the Specification and its parts, the Metaphysic and Couplings between systems, the Effect Grammar (the default architecture for broad systems), staged effects and Products, the Production Rule, the Residual Thaumaturgic Clause, agentive and shared Sources, Circulation and Carriers, Lenses, the Disclosure Profile, sealing the Specification, magic under the Resolution Protocol, Specification Validation, magical Drift, ceremony scaling, the Magic Menu and the Standard System

**Read when:** A Mode contains magic, advanced technology, or any other regime of effect beyond its setting's mundane baseline; a magical Attempt is resolved; a question about what magic can do comes up in Play; a Character's knowledge of magic is in doubt; a Player wants to choose the magic, or to take a ready-made system.

**Status:** Part of Draft 0.4. It builds on and adapts C. R. Rowenson, *The Magic-System Blueprint* (Rowenson Fiction, 2021). The concordance in §25.17 shows where each Blueprint component lives here.

---

## §25 Magic System Construction

### 25.1 Scope

**Magic**, for this section, is any regime of effect that departs from the setting's **Mundane Baseline**: the ordinary law the setting runs on when no magic is acting. The Baseline is Earth physics unless the Mode declares otherwise. Technology beyond that Baseline is magic for these purposes, which matches Rowenson's working definition ("anything enabling actions beyond our current capability or understanding") and his use of Clarke's Third Law together with its converse, which he calls "Agatha's Law": "Any sufficiently analyzed magic is indistinguishable from science."

Rowenson's definition is relative to a perspective: magic is beyond *someone's* capability or understanding. This section separates the two halves of it. Departure from the Baseline is a fact of the Specification (§25.4). Whether something is beyond a given entity's understanding is a fact about a Lens (§25.10).

A **magic system** is the complete set of rules that governs one regime of magic. A setting may hold several (§25.4.1).

### 25.2 The Author Hardness Principle

**[C]** Every magic system in a Mode is hard to its author.

Rowenson's hard/soft axis measures how much of a system is known or understood *from a chosen perspective*, and he applies it to the creator perspective as well: a creator building a large system can "get away with focusing on the general trends and specific pieces that will be important to the story," and "not every magic system needs to be a hard magic system; we don't need to know everything" (Part Three, *Hard and Soft Magic*). §25 does not import that allowance. Hard/soft is a problem that faces the audience and the player. It is a property of what someone inside the fiction, or the User, has been shown. It is never a property of the system as its author holds it.

The consequences:

1. **[C]** The author tier of a magic system, the **Specification**, is complete under the Closure Test (§25.3).
2. **[C]** Softness exists only in a Lens (§25.10) or in the Disclosure Profile (§25.11). A soft magic system is a fully specified system that someone has been shown little of.
3. **[C]** Irrationality relocates in the same way (§25.7). At the author tier, the question is not whether the system obeys logic but whether its effects are *generated* by a rule or *enumerated* in a closed catalogue. Both are hard.
4. **[C]** A magic system that the Operator "has in mind" but has not written down is Latent (§23.2), and a Latent Specification violates the Principle (§25.12).
5. **[C]** Hardness is not narrowness. The author of a broad system does not need to have written down every effect. The author needs a rule that decides every effect: whether it exists, what it costs, and what it can reach. A broad system that decides every request is as hard as a three-effect system that lists all three (§25.8).

Who counts as author follows the Authority Matrix. Material world facts are settled by Proposal and uptake under the Residual Authority Clause (§6.10, S5), so the default author is joint. A Mode in which the User wants to discover the magic usually allocates Specification authorship to the Operator, with the User's assent to that allocation and to the Disclosure Profile. The Principle binds whoever holds authorship, and it binds the User too when the User authors.

The reason it is constitutive and not a recommendation: the Operator resolves Attempts. An author who has not settled what magic can do will settle it at the moment of an Attempt, and at that moment the most probable continuation is whatever answer suits the scene. That is Accommodation (§18.3) by way of the magic system. Rowenson's discussion of the eagles in *The Lord of the Rings* names the effect as the audience sees it, a solution whose limits were never shown. In Play the cause runs deeper: the limits did not exist until they were needed. The Principle moves the answer to before the question, where Pre-commitment (§15) already puts the Odds.

### 25.3 The Closure Test

**[A]** A Specification is closed when every well-formed question about the magic's operation (*can this effect do this, to this, under these conditions, at what cost?*) receives one of three answers from it:

- **Determinate.** A fixed result, whether stated outright or validly derived.
- **Distributed.** A declared distribution over results (odds on the Ladder, §15, or a stated table), resolved by a declared randomness source (§15.9).
- **Prohibited.** It cannot.

"Not yet decided" is not an answer. A question the Specification cannot answer is a **Closure Failure** (§25.14).

The Closure Test cannot be run over every possible question, any more than Authority Closure can be run over every possible operation (§6.9). It is satisfied by construction through the Residual Thaumaturgic Clause (§25.6), and the Validation test is the checkable remainder: does any answer the Clause supplies contradict the Seed, the Mechanism Core, or the Purpose? (§25.13).

#### 25.3.1 Tendency Rules

Some rules of a setting govern populations, not instances. A talent sets an ordinary ceiling that most reach, some exceed and some fall short of. An age schedule describes the usual pace, and the setting may say outright that no one fits it exactly. Such a rule gives no Determinate answer for any one person, and leaving the individual case open would be a Closure Failure.

**[C]** A **Tendency Rule** is written in three lines:
- **Norm:** the ordinary outcome.
- **Deviation Table:** the share of cases that exceed the Norm and the share that fall short, and by how much.
- **Movers:** the named factors that shift those shares, each with its shift.

An individual's place in the table is drawn once from a declared randomness source when it first matters, entered in the Record, and never redrawn. Where the Authority Matrix gives the matter to a Party (the User's Character's own talent, under S2), that Party declares it instead, inside the table's range. A Tendency Rule answers the Closure Test as Distributed. Where the setting says that a rule "never perfectly fits", that sentence is the warrant for the Deviation Table. It is not an exemption from one.

#### 25.3.2 Discretion Points

A setting may place a ruling inside the fiction: a god chooses the trial, a sprite chooses whether to obey, a patron chooses whether to answer. Under the Author Hardness Principle, an in-world will that decides "at its discretion" is the Operator deciding at the moment of need, unless that discretion is written down.

**[C]** Every point at which the Specification defers to an in-world will is a **Discretion Point**, and it is closed by two things:
- the agent's **Stake Card** (§24.1), which fixes what it wants, what it will not do and what moves it;
- a **Discretion Table**, which maps the inputs the Specification names (the petitioner's standing, the offer made, the agent's present Want) to a result or a distribution over results.

The agent's choice in the fiction is the table's output. The will stays in the world, and the answer exists before the question. **[A]** A Discretion Point with no table is a Closure Failure, and resolving it ad hoc is Fiat Discretion (§25.15).

### 25.4 The Specification

**[C]** The Specification has six parts. Parts A to C are required for every magic system. Parts D to F scale with ceremony level (§25.16).

**A. Identity**
- **Name.** Rowenson's advice applies as given: the name is a reference handle, and an attributive working name ("viral magic") is sufficient.
- **Seed.** Rowenson's *seed crystal*: the single core concept the system grows from, in one line. It is the Specification's interpretive anchor, in the way the Intent Statement anchors the Mode (§4.1). A disputed entry is read toward the Seed.
- **Boundary.** What phenomena the system covers, and where it ends. This is the scope half of Rowenson's *Perspective* component, kept at the author tier.

**B. Mechanism Core.** The Blueprint has no counterpart to this part, because it describes how a system is experienced, not how it works (Rowenson's term for the variables is "the settings menu"). The Author Hardness Principle requires the workings themselves.
- **Substrate.** What magic is in the setting's ontology: a field, a substance, an entity, a contract, a pathogen, a property of names.
- **Operation.** The input, the process, and the output. What a user does, what the substrate does in response, and what results.
- **Invariants.** Laws that hold in every instance: conservation rules, equivalences, ratios. Rowenson's "Identify Themes, Patterns, and Equations" step belongs here when its products are true of the system, and in a Lens (§25.10) when they are only believed.
- **Prohibitions.** What the system never does, under any condition.
- **Interfaces.** How the system meets the Mundane Baseline (does magical fire behave as fire?), every other magic system in the setting, and its own countermeasures. Ways in which one system changes another system's numbers are Couplings, and they belong to the Metaphysic (§25.5).
- **Generativity.** Grammar, Rule, Enumerated, or Mixed (§25.7).

**C. Variables.** Rowenson's eight magic-system variables, kept under his names and set at the author tier: Transference, Prevalence, Source, Flux, Naturalness, Ease of Use, Reliability, Consistency. They are anchored in §25.9. Each takes a value and a one-line reason. Where Lens values differ from the Specification values, the Lens records them, not the Specification.

**D. Effect Architecture.** How the system decides which effects exist. There are two forms:
- **Effect Grammar (§25.8).** This is the default, and it is required wherever the system is broad: one with more effects than can be listed in advance, or one in which users compose new effects. The Grammar's Benchmarks and its accreting Codex are written on Effect Cards.
- **Effects Catalogue.** Every effect, each on an Effect Card (§25.4.2). This is used for narrow systems, meaning a Rule system with a handful of expected derivations or an Enumerated system. In an Enumerated system the Catalogue is the system.

**E. Limitations.** Rowenson's "three C's", kept as given:
- **Costs:** what is paid or spent to produce an effect.
- **Consequences:** what follows from producing it, direct or indirect.
- **Countermeasures:** what can counteract or cancel it, magical or mundane.

Capability ceilings, meaning things the system could in principle do but cannot do at a given scale, are also recorded here.

**F. Balance Record.** The result of the Boundedness audit (§25.13), recording every disparity the system creates and every Unbounded State, each closed or else kept on purpose.

Rowenson's *Perception* component is not part of the Specification. How the magic looks, sounds, tastes and feels when it is used is a fact, so it goes on each Effect Card as its **Signature**. What people think of the magic (good or evil, desirable or not, the standing of its users) is held by a population, so it goes in a Lens.

Rowenson's *Align It with Your Story* step is not imported. A Mode elects toward its Purpose Profile and its declared Narrative Organization (§4.3, §11). A Specification is validated against its Seed, its Mechanism Core and the Purpose. It is never judged by what story it could produce.

#### 25.4.1 System Boundaries

**[B]** A system is drawn around its Substrate: the medium in which its effects are produced. Effects produced in the same medium belong to one Specification, however unlike each other they are. Effects produced in different media belong to separate Specifications, even where they spend the same fuel, rank on the same ladder or are learned the same way. What several systems share (a fuel, a scale, a medium of knowledge, a law) belongs to the Metaphysic (§25.5), not to any one of them.

Rowenson treats the whole Phase One cast of the Marvel films as a single system for analysis because they appear together. Under §25 that grouping is a Lens's view. The author tier asks where each effect is produced.

#### 25.4.2 The Effect Card

**[S]** Each Benchmark, each Codex entry, and each catalogued effect carries a card of no more than ten lines. A Grammar system adds two lines: **Domain** and **Tier**, with the Price computed by the Cost Function (§25.8).

- **Effect.** What happens, stated as Operation: what is done, then the result.
- **Parameters.** Rowenson's eight balance mechanics, set per effect: Frequency (how often), Duration, Magnitude, Area, Range, Efficiency (yield per unit of Source), Malleability (how much the user can shape the preceding six), and Success (the base rung on the Odds Ladder, §15).
- **Cost.** Paid on every use, or under stated conditions.
- **Consequence.** What follows, and when.
- **Counters.** What stops or degrades it.
- **Signature.** How it manifests to the senses.
- **Gate Ceiling.** The highest Consequence Gate Tier (§7.6) the effect can produce by itself. An effect whose ceiling is Tier 3 is admissible only where the Instrument declares Tier 3 admissible.

Rowenson's balance mechanics become Parameters for a reason. A Parameter written before Play is a pre-commitment about magnitude, in the way a Stake Card's Price is a pre-commitment about yielding (§24.1). An effect whose range or duration is settled when it is first used will be settled at whatever the scene wants.

### 25.5 The Metaphysic and Coupled Systems

A setting with one magic system needs no Metaphysic. A setting with several usually does, because its systems are rarely independent. They draw on a common fuel, rank on a common ladder, trade power at declared rates, and change one another's variables. Where that is so, the shared layer is where much of the setting's magic actually lives. A set of Specifications without it is closed within each system and open between them.

**[C]** Where two or more Specifications share anything beyond the Mundane Baseline, they sit under a **Metaphysic**. It is sealed with them (§25.12) and has six parts.

1. **Shared Resources.** Each energy, medium or quantity that more than one system draws on (a life-energy, a mental energy, a fuel), with its own Source entry (§25.9) and the list of systems that draw on it.
2. **Common Scale.** Where the setting ranks many things on one ladder, the ladder and its **concordance**: one row per rung, one column per ranked thing (realms, technique ranks, material grades, creature grades, institutional ranks). **[C]** A thing ranked on the Common Scale carries the rung's meaning into every system, unless its own Specification declares otherwise. The concordance is how a question in one system ("can a Gold vessel hold this?") is answered from another ("this is a Tier IV effect, which sits at Adamantine").
3. **Equivalences.** Declared exchange rates between the measures of different systems: one system's rank stated as equal to another's, or a unit of one energy stated as worth some quantity of another. **[A]** Equivalences compose. If A equals B and B equals C, then A equals C, and a Specification that implies otherwise fails Metaphysic coherence (§25.13).
4. **Substitution Rules.** Where one resource can stand in for another: the conditions, the rate, and what is lost in the exchange.
5. **Couplings.** Every way one system acts on another's variables or Parameters: a draught that multiplies a replenishment rate, a metal that suppresses a casting, a capability required before an oath can be sworn. Each Coupling is one row: **source system → target system · what it modifies · by how much · under what conditions · how it stacks.** An Interface entry (§25.4 B) states how two systems meet. A Coupling changes a number in the other system.
6. **Shared Carriers.** Where the knowledge or capacity to use magic is held in something common to several systems (a script, an organ, a living carrier), the Carrier Rule for it (§25.10.2).

**[C] Stacking.** Couplings of the same kind on the same target do not stack unless their row says they do: the strongest applies. Where distinct Couplings apply together, multiplicative modifiers multiply and additive modifiers add, unless a row declares otherwise. A Coupling that would carry a target past its own Envelope or Apex is capped there.

**[C] One reserve.** Where several systems draw on one resource, a single reserve is tracked, not one for each system. Spending it in one system leaves less for all the others.

### 25.6 The Residual Thaumaturgic Clause

**[C]** Whatever the Specification does not address is resolved as follows. The Clause does for magic what the Residual Authority Clause (§6.10) does for operations: it makes closure hold by construction, so that a Specification of moderate length is still complete.

1. **Closure of Effects.** In a Grammar system: an effect exists if and only if it lies within a Domain and inside the Envelope of a Tier the user can access, and it costs what the Cost Function computes for it. Anything outside every Domain, or above the Apex, does not happen. In a Catalogue system, no effect exists beyond those catalogued or validly derived from the Rule (the **Closed Catalogue**).
2. **Condition Invariance.** A condition that the Specification does not name does not modify an effect. A spell that is not said to fail in rain works in rain.
3. **Mundane Continuity.** Outside its declared effects, magic obeys the Mundane Baseline, and the products of magic behave as their mundane counterparts do. Magical fire burns, heats and consumes fuel like fire, unless its Effect Card says otherwise. This is Rowenson's two-button example (red button, flames) made a default: the "logical assumptions a user might make" hold unless the Specification breaks them.
4. **Strict Cost.** Costs and Consequences apply in full every time their conditions are met. None are waived because they are inconvenient.
5. **Coupling Closure.** No system modifies another except through a declared Coupling (§25.5). Where two systems meet with no Interface entry and no Coupling, neither modifies the other, and each obeys the Mundane Baseline with respect to the other's products.

**[A]** A Specification may displace any line of the Clause for itself, and records the displacement. A system in which unlisted conditions *do* matter (weather-bound magic, star-timed ritual) must enumerate those conditions or state a generator for them.

### 25.7 Generativity: Rowenson's rational/irrational axis at the author tier

Rowenson's rational/irrational axis measures whether "people can use what they already know to logically predict unseen functions, rules, or applications." He places irrationality in the system itself: in an irrational system, someone "knows something is true only when they see or in the creator's case, decide that it is true." Under the Author Hardness Principle, the author's half of that clause is a Latent system, and the Principle does not admit it. The axis is therefore split in two.

**At the author tier, Generativity (categorical):**
- **Grammar.** A structured generator (Domains, Tiers, a Cost Function, and Access) produces and prices an open-ended space of effects. This is the default form for broad systems (§25.8).
- **Rule.** A single law produces the effects (the worked example SPEC-Brine: "the salt obeys the word"). A valid extrapolation from the law binds, and the Catalogue is a convenience.
- **Enumerated.** A closed list is the system, and no rule connects its entries. Extrapolation from one entry to an unlisted one fails under the Closed Catalogue. The system is still hard: every answer exists, but no answer can be inferred.
- **Mixed.** Grammar or Rule within declared families, Enumerated between them. (Rowenson's reading of *Stargate SG-1*: devices that each work rationally, joined by shared power sources.)

**At the Lens tier, Apparent Rationality (0 to 4, §25.10):** whether a perspective can extrapolate correctly. A Grammar or Rule system whose generator is hidden appears irrational to that perspective. An Enumerated system with a strong sensory or thematic Signature can appear rational, which is Rowenson's point about the red mist and the taste of rotten peaches producing a sense of connection "even if there isn't." That impression belongs to a Lens. The theory the impression produces is a Claim (§16.8).

**[A]** In a Grammar or Rule system, an Attempt that extrapolates correctly succeeds or fails on the merits of the derivation. In an Enumerated system, it fails. That failure is the Specification working as written, and it is not Resistance.

### 25.8 The Effect Grammar

Most magic systems that Modes host are broad: tabletop spell systems with hundreds of spells across schools and levels, isekai systems of skills, ranks and mana in which users invent new techniques, cultivation ladders, and elemental schools whose users improvise. No author lists every effect such a system can produce. The Author Hardness Principle does not ask for a list. It asks that every request receive an answer, and a Grammar is a fixed procedure that gives one. The model is a legal code, which does not list every case and still decides each one (like in Earth IRL a tax schedule, which prices every income without naming any taxpayer).

**[C]** An Effect Grammar has eight components.

1. **Domains.** The partition of what the magic can touch: schools, elements, affinities, skill trees, aspects. Each Domain has a one-line scope and its own Prohibitions. A request that lies in no Domain is Prohibited. A request that spans several Domains is priced in each and pays the Combination Rule's surcharge.
   - **Domain Genesis.** Some settings let Domains come into being, change hands or lapse during Play: a field of reality becomes workable once enough attention gathers on it, a school is founded, an element is discovered. Such a Grammar states its **Genesis Rule**: the condition under which a Domain enters, the condition under which it leaves, and what holds it in the meantime. The live list of Domains is a **Domain Registry**, kept in the Record. **[C]** A Domain that enters under the Genesis Rule is a Derivation, not an Extension, because the Grammar already said it would.
2. **Power Axes.** One or more ordered axes on which effects are ranked, each with its own **gating stat**: the property of the user that unlocks it. Many systems have a single axis (spell levels 0 to 9, ranks F to S, realms of cultivation). Others separate *magnitude* (how much an effect does) from *complexity* (how intricate it may be), and some add *proficiency* (how well this user performs it). Each of these may be gated by a different property of the user. A point on the primary axis is a **Tier**, and each Tier carries an **Envelope**: the ceiling on each Parameter (Magnitude, Area, Range, Duration, number of targets) and a **Gate Ceiling** (§7.6). A point on a secondary axis is a **Grade**, and each Grade states what it adds (a more complex effect, better efficiency, a special ability) and what it requires. Envelopes and Grades are tables, not descriptions.
3. **Cost Function.** The Price of an effect as a function of its Tier, its Grade and its declared Modifiers. **The Price is a vector.** It names every resource the effect spends (slots, mana, stamina, a life-energy, a mental energy, components, casting time, concentration, lifespan) and the quantity of each. Where an effect spends at more than one moment (§25.8.4), the Price is divided across those moments. **[C]** The function is computable. Given a Tier, a Grade and Modifiers, two Operators compute the same Price.
4. **Modifier Table.** Standard adjustments (extended range, wider area, longer duration, silent casting, added targets), each with its Price change and its effect on Tier. Modifiers are how a user shapes an effect, and they are where Rowenson's *Malleability* lives in a Grammar.
5. **Access Rule.** Who can reach which Domains at which Tiers and Grades, and how access grows: class and level tables, skill acquisition, affinity at birth, cultivation breakthroughs. The **Progression Rule** is Flux at the scale of the individual (§25.14.3). Where progression is a tendency and not a law, it is written as a Tendency Rule (§25.3.1).
   - **[C] Gate kinds.** Each gate in the Access Rule is marked with its kind. A **Metaphysical** gate is a fact of the Specification. It holds in every society the setting could contain, and only Amendment changes it. An **Institutional** gate is a fact about the setting's societies: a guild's admission rule, a law, a rank ladder, a ban. It can be broken, evaded, reformed or abolished inside the fiction, and breaching it has institutional consequences, not metaphysical ones. A gate that follows from other gates (no one outside a group can practise because practice requires a capability only that group has) is marked **Derived**, with the gates it follows from. It is Metaphysical only if every gate it follows from is Metaphysical.
6. **Benchmarks.** Fully carded anchor effects that calibrate each Tier: at Light ceremony, one for every two or three Tiers; at Full ceremony, at least one per Tier in each principal Domain. **[C]** Benchmark Parity: no derived effect at a Tier exceeds that Tier's Benchmarks in aggregate value. Where it would, it moves up a Tier. Benchmarks are the Grammar's precedent. They stop the Envelope's ceilings being read as a free choice of any point beneath them.
7. **Combination Rule.** How effects stack, chain and compose: concentration limits, the number of active effects, whether like effects stack, action economy, the surcharge for multi-Domain effects. Breadth makes this the main site of Unbounded States (§25.13).
8. **Apex Rule.** What the top Tier can do, and what even the top Tier cannot do. This is where omnipotence is closed for the whole system at once, instead of effect by effect.

#### 25.8.1 The Pricing Procedure

**[C]** A requested effect that is not yet in the Codex is priced in this order, and **before** its Odds are stated (§15), so that the Price is pre-committed like the Stakes:

1. **Domain.** Find the Domain or Domains it lies in. None means Prohibited.
2. **Placement.** Place each Parameter against the Envelopes. The effect's Tier is the lowest Tier whose Envelope contains every Parameter. Where the Grammar has secondary axes, place the effect's complexity on them in the same way. **Ties and ambiguity round up, on every axis.** A placement that could reasonably fall at either of two Tiers takes the higher one, because the Operator's disposition to please pulls placement downward.
3. **Parity.** Compare it with the Benchmarks at that Tier. If it exceeds them in aggregate, it moves up one Tier and is compared again.
4. **Access.** Check that the user can reach that Domain at that Tier. If not, the effect cannot be cast by this user. It still exists in the system.
5. **Price.** Compute the Price vector from the Cost Function, the Grade and the Modifiers.
6. **Codex.** Enter the effect in the Codex with its Domain, Tier, Grade, Price, and the Benchmark it was compared against.

The result is a **Derivation** (§25.14.2): it was true of the system before anyone asked, and it is binding once found. A Codex entry is reused exactly on every later casting, so the same request is never priced twice.

**[B]** Where the User invents a technique (a common move in isekai Play, where a protagonist's outside knowledge produces a new application), the invention is a Move, and the Grammar prices it. Ingenuity counts in two ways. It can find a cheaper *valid* placement: the same goal by a lower-Tier route the Envelope allows. It can also count as an Established factor on the Odds where the Specification names comprehension as one. It never lowers a Price below what the Cost Function gives for the effect as placed.

**[S]** Where Transparency is below 3, pricing is done silently and recorded in the Codex. The User can audit it under Revelation (§23.4).

#### 25.8.2 Diegetic Interfaces

Many broad systems include an in-world interface to the Grammar itself: status windows, skill readouts, a divine System's notifications, a sect's rank tablets. **[C]** The Specification declares the Interface's **Authority**:
- **Authoritative.** What it displays is a Specification fact, disclosed to whoever reads it. It raises that reader's Lens directly.
- **Fallible.** What it displays is a Claim, with a declared rate or pattern of error.
- **Partisan.** It is accurate but incomplete in a declared direction, for example concealing the System's own Apex or its makers' purposes.

An interface need not be an artefact. A perceptible sign that the Specification makes reliable (an aura that cannot be falsified, a colour that shows a charge, a trail that marks where power was spent) is an Authoritative interface in the same sense, available to whoever can perceive it. An Authoritative interface is a Disclosure channel, and the Disclosure Profile accounts for it. A Target Hardness of 1 is incompatible with a status screen that shows full skill descriptions.

#### 25.8.3 Grammar and Catalogue together

**[B]** Many systems are a Grammar with exceptions: a few **Signature Effects** (unique skills, artefact powers, cheat abilities, a bloodline's single gift) that sit outside the Cost Function. Each is carded in full, carries its own Price, and is entered in the Balance Record as a Retained Disparity or closed by its own Limitation. A Signature Effect never serves as a Benchmark.

#### 25.8.4 Staged Effects and Products

Many systems separate making an effect from releasing it. A spell is formed slowly and stored, then cast in a moment. A technique must be activated by a rite before it can be used. A ward is inscribed and triggers later. A formula is brewed, then drunk.

**[C]** A staged effect declares its **Stages**: usually Preparation and Release, and in some systems Activation or Maintenance as well. For each Stage it states the Price, the time taken, who may perform it, whether several performers can share it, and what can interrupt it. The Price vector is divided across the Stages as the system says. A spell may carry its whole burden at forming and none at casting.

**[C]** Whatever Preparation produces and Release consumes is a **Product**: a stored spell, a charged object, an inscribed ward, a brewed draught. Each type of Product has a short entry:
- **Vessel:** what holds it, and any grade limit on what the vessel can hold.
- **Trigger:** who or what can release it: its maker only, anyone who holds it, anyone near it who knows how, or a condition.
- **Shelf:** its stability, and how it decays.
- **Uses:** one, or many.
- **Transferability:** the variable of §25.9, set for the Product separately from the capability that made it. Systems whose capability is bound and whose Products move freely are common, and the two values are recorded apart.

**[S]** Products in circulation are inventory. Wherever they matter to Play, the Record tracks who holds what, at what grade, and with how much shelf left.

**[A]** At Resolution (§25.14.1), releasing a Product is resolved with the Odds and Price of its Release Stage only. The Preparation was resolved when the Product was made.

#### 25.8.5 The Production Rule

Crafting systems (alchemy, enchanting, artifice, breeding, forging) resolve requests for Products, not for effects. A Grammar prices an effect, and a **Production Rule** prices and grades a Product.

**[C]** A Production Rule declares:
- **Inputs:** the classes of input, each with its own grade scale and condition scale (fresh, aged, degraded).
- **Processes:** the operations available, and what each does to the inputs.
- **Producer:** the producer's rank and how it is attained, and the tools or station required.
- **Output Space:** the Product's attributes and their scales (grade, category, potency, duration, stability, side effects, legality).
- **Grade Function:** the output grade as a function of the input grades, the process and the producer's rank. It gives the highest grade these inputs and this producer can reach, and the distribution of results beneath it.
- **Side-Effect Function:** how purity, toxicity or flaws are determined, with their table.
- **Failure:** what a failed production loses, or produces instead.

**[C]** A requested Product not yet in the Codex is graded by the Production Rule and entered there, as an effect is priced by the Pricing Procedure. The outcome of a particular production is drawn from the Grade Function's distribution under the Resolution Protocol, with the producer's rank and the inputs as named Odds factors.

### 25.9 Anchored Scales for the Variables

Each anchor describes what is true of the system at that value. Values 1 and 3 are interpolations, as in §8.7. Two variables are categorical.

| Variable | 0 | 2 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Transference** | Bound: the capability is fixed to its holder at origin; it cannot be gained, lent, lost or taken | Conditional: gained or lost through a specified process of real cost or rarity; temporary transfer possible under stated conditions | Free: the capability resides in a transferable object or freely acquired state; possession confers access |
| **Prevalence** | Singular or absent within the Boundary | Uncommon: present in a minority of the population or places; most inhabitants know of it and rarely meet it | Pervasive: part of most inhabitants' daily life, or of the ambient environment |
| **Naturalness** | Aberrant: breaks the setting's other regularities; never occurs unprompted | Partly integrated: obeys the Baseline at its Interfaces; some occurrence in creatures, materials or places | Constitutive: part of the setting's natural law; occurs unprompted in creatures, materials and weather |
| **Ease of Use** | Effortless: passive or by thought; no training, components or time | Trained: months or years of training, or components to hand, or casting time from seconds to minutes | Arduous: years of training, rare components, long rites, unbroken focus; failure is dangerous |
| **Reliability** | Gamble: the outcome is undetermined until drawn, in both whether it happens and what happens | Mostly dependable: a stated failure chance or variance, and stated countermeasures that interrupt it | Certain: the same input yields the same output, with no failure and no countermeasure short of a Prohibition |
| **Consistency** | Singular: every user and instance differs in effect, manner and variable settings | Familial: shared mechanism; effect-set or manner varies by declared class; deviations enumerated | Uniform: identical effects, manner and settings across all users and instances |
| **Source** (categorical) | Locus: internal, external, **agentive** (the power is lent by a being with a will), or mixed by effect. Reserve: finite, renewable, infinite, or **shared** (one pool held by several parties in shares). A **Renewal Rule** (what refills it, how fast) is required for renewable reserves, and a **Depletion Effect** (what happens at zero) for finite and renewable ones. An agentive Source requires the patron's Stake Card and its **Terms**; a shared Source requires a **Share Ledger** | | |
| **Flux** (categorical) | Sign: negative, neutral, or positive. A **Driver** (what is moving the quantity) and a **Measure** (a Clock, §15.8, or a Record count) are required for a non-neutral sign | | |

Ease of Use is a scale of difficulty. Its 0 is the easiest, and it keeps Rowenson's name.

**[C]** Reliability, Consistency and Source carry their meanings as distributions where they are not 4. A Reliability of 2 names its failure chance (a Ladder rung or a table) and its variance (a table of alternative results). Under No Counterfeit Randomness (§20.14), a distributed result is resolved by a declared randomness source and never by an authored number presented as chance. A Wild Magic system is Reliability 0 or 1 with a declared table and a real draw. It is not an unspecified system.

**[C] Agentive and shared Sources.** A patron who lends power is an Operator Character with a Want, a Line and a Price, not a reservoir. Its **Terms** state what the user owes and what follows on default. Whether it keeps lending is settled by its Stake Card and its Terms, never by what the scene needs, and the Price Test (§24.2) applies: a patron that gives more than its Terms provide must be shown to have had its Price met. A shared Source's **Share Ledger** records who holds what share, and the shares move only on declared causes, recorded as they occur. Where shares are contested, the contest is a set of Clocks, one for each contender.

**[C]** Consistency below 4 enumerates its deviations. "Some practitioners can do more" is not a value. "Practitioners of the Hollow line add Effect 7; no others can" is.

Rowenson also lists the main dependencies between variables ("Blueprint Connections"). Under §25 they are **[A]** coherence checks for Validation (§25.13), not rules. A Specification may set two variables against their usual pull if it records why. His main stated connections:
- external Source tends to raise Transference;
- Prevalence raises Lens hardness and Apparent Rationality;
- Flux measures the rate of change of Prevalence;
- Naturalness tends to raise rationality;
- understanding raises Reliability only up to the system's own variance (his die example).

### 25.10 Lenses

Rowenson's *Perspective* component, and his Part Four practice of mapping further Blueprints from the universal, audience, protagonist and antagonist perspectives, become Lenses. A **Lens** is the magic system as one perspective inside the fiction holds it: a Character, a faction, a culture, a profession.

**[S]** A Lens Sheet records:
- **Holder.** Whose Lens this is.
- **Known.** Which Specification entries the holder knows, each with its in-fiction source. This is the Knowledge Matrix row for magic (§16.9), kept in one place.
- **Believed.** What the holder believes that the Specification does not support, or that it contradicts: theories, doctrine, superstition, propaganda. Each entry is a **Claim** (§16.8), never a Specification fact.
- **Apparent values.** Hardness (below), Apparent Rationality (0 to 4), and any of the eight variables where the holder's estimate differs from the Specification. Rowenson's examples: Allomancy is hard for the reader, less hard for a noble of the Great Houses, and soft for a skaa labourer.
- **Perception.** Valuation (sacred, criminal, mundane, feared), the standing of users and of those who lack the magic, and taboos.

**Lens Hardness (0 to 4):**

| 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware that the magic exists | Aware of its existence; knows of effects by report only | Knows some effects and some limits first-hand or by reliable teaching | Knows most effects, limits and the Operation | Holds the Specification in full |

**[C]** Only the author tier holds Hardness 4 by right. A Character at 4 is a declared fact of the Specification (a god, a founder, a perfect archive). That Character is subject to the Boundedness audit (omniscience, §25.13).

**[C]** Characters act on their Lens, never on the Specification. A Character who uses magic knowledge their Lens does not record has failed the Source Test (§16.9), and the failure is a **Lens Leak** (§25.15). What the Operator knows as author confers nothing on any Character (§23.5).

**[C]** A false Believed entry is never corrected by the world to fit the belief. When a Character acts on a false theory, the Specification decides what happens, and the result is evidence the Character may learn from. This is how Lens Hardness rises in Play: through Rowenson's mechanism of exposure and experiment, carried out by Characters inside the fiction. Under a Carrier Rule (§25.10.2), it is also how Lens Hardness falls.

#### 25.10.1 Content and Circulation

Some settings make belief itself a cause. Attention, reputation, worship or song feeds a power, and the power fades when the story is forgotten. This does not breach the Claim Layer, because two different properties of a belief are at work.

**[C]** The **content** of a belief is always a Claim. A song that says a hero slew a dragon does not make it true that he did. The **circulation** of a belief (how many hold it, how intensely, how far it travels, and through what medium) is a fact of the world, and the Specification may declare circulation a cause of magical effect. Where it does, it states a **Circulation Rule**: what is measured, how it is measured (a Clock, a Record count, a graded scale), what it feeds, and what moves it (propagation, suppression, erasure, rival stories).

Under a Circulation Rule, a Character may change the world by changing what is believed, within the rule. The world still never bends to a belief's content: a power may grow because more people sing of its victory, and the victory does not thereby become history. **[C]** Where a Specification does declare that content becomes true once circulation passes a threshold (belief made fact), that is a Genesis Rule over facts, and it is stated as one: the threshold, the scope of what becomes true, and from what moment. Without such a rule, content stays a Claim.

#### 25.10.2 Carriers of Knowledge

A Lens records what a holder knows. Some settings put that knowledge in a thing: a living creature that is the technique, a rune that must be carried, an organ that holds a spell, a book that teaches only the one who holds it. Knowledge held this way can be stolen, traded, lost, killed or inherited, and losing the thing loses the knowledge.

**[C]** A **Carrier Rule** declares:
- what carries the knowledge;
- how it is acquired (learned, captured, made, inherited);
- what keeps it (feeding, storage, upkeep), and what that costs;
- how it is lost (death, escape, destruction, decay), and what the holder keeps after the loss;
- whether it can be transferred, and how;
- whether it has a will.

**[C]** Where knowledge has a Carrier, each entry in a Lens's *Known* list names the carrier that holds it, and the entry is struck when that carrier is lost. Every such loss is recorded with its cause. A Carrier with a will, at a sapience where its choices matter, has a Stake Card, and its cooperation is a Discretion Point (§25.3.2). The table that closes it usually reads the holder's proficiency or bond.

### 25.11 The Disclosure Profile

The Disclosure Profile is where the hard/soft dichotomy lives: how hard the system is *to the User*, which is a separate question from how hard it is to the User's Character.

**[S]** The Profile declares:
- **Target Hardness.** The User-facing Hardness the Mode aims at (0 to 4, on the Lens scale), at opening and over the horizon. "Soft magic" is a Target Hardness of 1 or 2 held steady. "Hard magic" is 3 or 4 reached early. Rowenson's account of Mistborn, where a single training chapter discloses most of the system, is an early climb to 3.
- **Disclosure Orientation.** Earned, triggered, or free (§8.3). Earned disclosure arrives only through the User's Character's experience and the Lenses it can reach.
- **Resolution Transparency.** For magical Attempts, whether Odds, Costs and Consequences are shown before the draw. The default follows the Mode's Transparency (§8.4). A Mode that hides magical Odds while showing mundane ones says so here.
- **Exposition Bound.** The most the Operator may explain about magic outside a Lens. The default is nothing: magic is explained only by a Character, inside the fiction, and only from what that Character's Lens holds.

**[C]** Soft Disclosure never licenses unspecified resolution. Where the User's Character attempts something with magic that the User has never been shown, the Operator resolves it from the Specification. The User's ignorance changes what the User is told, and never what is true.

**Competence Delegation.** Rowenson notes that a system can be hard to a Character and soft to the reader, through "narrative coverage" of the Character's knowledge. In Play, when the User's Character's Lens is harder than the User's Disclosure (a trained mage played by a User who wants to discover the magic), **[C]** the Mode records in its Authority Matrix (§6.11) that the User's Character's magical knowledge, part of S2, is delegated to the Operator. The User declares intent ("I ward the door against what's hunting us"). The Operator renders the Character's competent execution from that Character's Lens and discloses only what the execution shows. The delegation covers knowledge and craft. It never covers the choice to act (S1), which stays with the User.

In the reverse case, where the User's Disclosure is harder than their Character's Lens, **[B]** the User undertakes that the Character acts only on the Character's Lens. This is the User-side counterpart of the Source Test, and it is the User's discipline to keep, under the Knowledge Licence (§8.3).

### 25.12 Sealing the Specification

The Specification is hidden state wherever Disclosure is below 4, and it binds Play for longer than any context window lasts. Under §23.1 it therefore enters the Record at a declared Commitment Level.

**[C]** The Mechanism Core, the Variables, the Catalogue and the Limitations are never held at Level 0, Latent. This is the Author Hardness Principle expressed in the Commitment Ladder. An Operator's reasoning does not persist from turn to turn, so a system held "in mind" and not written down is re-derived on every continuation and pulled toward each scene's convenience. Only a written Specification is a fixed one.

| Capability | Level | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code execution and files | 3, Hash Seal | Write the Specification to a file in the Record directory; run `python scripts/seal.py make SPEC-<name> "$(cat <spec-file>)" --dir <record-dir>`; publish the digest only. Each Specification Amendment is resealed, and the old digest is kept in the Record |
| Files or a persistent memory store, no code | 2, Held Seal | Write the Specification to a store that the User undertakes not to read |
| Neither | 2, transcript variant, or Disclosure raised | Write the Specification into the transcript in a clearly marked sealed block that the User undertakes not to read. This is a Held Seal whose store is the transcript itself: it persists only as long as the transcript does, and it is declared as such. Otherwise, accept a Target Hardness that permits full disclosure. If neither is acceptable, the Mode fails Capability feasibility (§10.5) |

**[B]** A **Specification Marker** may be published at any Level: a short public statement of properties (for example, "SPEC-Vael: it costs the caster something every time; nothing comes back from death; it cannot read minds"). It works like a Level 1 Commitment Marker for the published lines. It raises the User's Disclosure by exactly what it states and by nothing more, and the Disclosure Profile records it.

**[C]** Revelation is checked at the sealed Level, as §23.4 requires: when the User asks, or at Mode Closure, the Specification is opened and compared against every magical outcome in Canon.

### 25.13 Specification Validation

**[C]** Specification Validation runs inside the Mode's Validation (§10.5) as part of Compatibility and Capability feasibility. It is conjunctive, like §10.5: one failure rejects or amends the candidate.

- **Closure.** No answer supplied by the Residual Thaumaturgic Clause contradicts the Seed, the Mechanism Core or the Purpose. Every Distributed variable names its distribution and its randomness source.
- **Invariant consistency.** No two entries contradict each other. No Effect Card breaks an Invariant or a Prohibition unless an exception is recorded on both.
- **Interface completeness.** Each Interface is declared, or deliberately left to Coupling Closure. Every Countermeasure listed on an Effect Card exists in the Specification or the Baseline.
- **Boundedness.** Rowenson's *Break, Inspect, and Repair* step, kept with his four failure states: **omnipotence, omniscience, omnipresence, infinite wealth**. For each, the auditor asks whether any route through the Specification (any effect, combination, feedback loop or exploit) leads a user to that state. Each route found is either closed (a Limitation, a Prohibition, a Parameter cap) or recorded in the Balance Record as a **Retained Disparity**, with who can reach it and at what cost. Rowenson also recommends treating the system as real and asking how one would exploit it, or handing it to a min-maxer. Under §25 that check is mandatory. Where a second agent is available, it is done by an External Auditor (§18.7) who has not seen the Specification being written.
- **Disparity mapping.** For the Balance Record: does an average user hold an extreme advantage, physical, social or financial, over a non-user? Rowenson asks this question as his starting test. The answer is recorded as a systemic fact about the setting: who holds power because of the magic, and what checks it.
- **Lens consistency.** Every Lens entry is either a Specification fact with a source or a Claim. No Lens holds Hardness 4 without a Specification entry granting it.
- **Disclosure feasibility.** The Commitment Level that §25.12 requires for the Target Hardness is available here.
- **Gate fit.** No Effect Card's Gate Ceiling, and no Tier's Envelope, exceeds what the Instrument admits (§7.6).
- **Grammar determinacy** (Grammar systems). The Cost Function is computable; every Tier has an Envelope table; the Benchmarks fall inside their own Envelopes and rise monotonically in value from Tier to Tier; every Modifier states both its Price change and its Tier effect. Test: price three requests the author did not anticipate, one low, one middling and one near the Apex. Each must come out at one Price without any judgment beyond Placement.
- **Metaphysic coherence** (several systems). No rung of the Common Scale carries two meanings. Equivalences compose. Every Coupling names its target, its magnitude, its conditions and its stacking. Every shared resource has one reserve.
- **Discretion closure.** Every Discretion Point has a Stake Card and a Discretion Table.
- **Tendency form.** Every Tendency Rule has a Norm, a Deviation Table and its Movers.
- **Stage and Product completeness.** Every staged effect prices each of its Stages. Every Product type has a Vessel, Trigger, Shelf, Uses and Transferability.
- **Production determinacy** (crafting systems). Grade three Products the author did not anticipate, as in the Grammar determinacy test. Each must come out at one grade distribution without judgment beyond the Production Rule.
- **Gate classification.** Every Access gate is marked Metaphysical, Institutional or Derived, and every Derived gate names the gates it follows from.
- **Genesis and Ledger.** Where Domains, shares or circulation can change in Play, the Genesis Rule, the Share Ledger and the Circulation Rule exist, and every cause that moves them is declared.
- **Combinatorial bound** (Grammar systems). Boundedness is run on the Grammar, not effect by effect: on stacking, chaining, loops that turn effects into Source or wealth (create, then sell; summon, then sacrifice), and the Apex. In a broad system the four Unbounded States are reached by combination far more often than by any single effect.

The Boundedness audit is a check on the closure and coherence of the system. It does not ask whether a disparity is good or bad for a story. A Retained Disparity is a fact about the world, and Operator Characters reckon with it in the Mode's world as they would with any other standing fact: through their Stake Cards, their Leverage and their Lines.

### 25.14 Magic in Play

#### 25.14.1 Magical Attempts

**[C]** A magical Attempt at Tier 1 or above follows the Resolution Protocol (§15), and the Specification supplies its terms before the draw:

1. **Stakes.** Success yields the Effect Card's or Codex entry's result at its Parameters. In a Grammar system, an uncodexed effect is first priced (§25.8.1). Failure yields what the card and the Reliability distribution declare. Cost is paid in either case unless the card says otherwise, and Consequences attach on their stated conditions.
2. **Odds.** Start at the card's Success rung (in a Grammar, the Tier's default rung, adjusted by how far the Tier sits above or below the user's Access), not at Even. Move one rung for each named Established factor, Ease of Use requirements met or unmet among them (training, components, time, focus). A countermeasure in play moves the rung as its entry states.
3. **Draw** from the declared source. Render within the Outcome Band, as follows:
   - **Clean Success:** the effect at its Parameters, Cost paid.
   - **Success at Cost:** the effect, plus a Consequence or a Parameter shortfall drawn from the card.
   - **Failure with Opening:** no effect, Cost paid; the failure reveals something true about the Specification. This is the main way earned Disclosure arrives.
   - **Clean Failure:** no effect, Cost paid, and any failure Consequence the card declares.

The bands draw their content from the card. They are never invented at the moment of rendering.

**[C]** Tier 0 magical acts (a Signature flourish, a cantrip with no stake) are rendered unilaterally by the Party holding the operation, within the Specification. Strict Cost still applies.

#### 25.14.2 Derivation and Extension

When Play raises a question the written Specification does not answer in so many words, the Operator determines which kind of question it is.

- **[C] Derivation.** The answer follows from the Specification and the Residual Thaumaturgic Clause. In a Grammar system, every effect priced by the Pricing Procedure (§25.8.1) is a Derivation. So is every Domain admitted under a Genesis Rule, every Product graded by a Production Rule, every individual's place drawn under a Tendency Rule, and every in-world choice read from a Discretion Table. It is binding at once. It is entered in the Rulings Register (§3.10) with its derivation, and it is not an Amendment: the answer was already true and has now been found. A ruling is a Derivation only if it passes the Clarification Test (§17.2): no continuation already produced in the Part would be judged differently under it.
- **[C] Extension.** The answer requires a fact that the Specification does not contain and cannot generate. In a Grammar system, that means a new Domain, a new Tier, a change to an Envelope, the Cost Function or a Modifier, or a Placement the Grammar cannot settle. This is a Closure Failure. The immediate Attempt is resolved by an Oracle Question (§15.10) at Odds fixed by the Ladder alone, so that the Operator's preference about the answer is excluded. The ruling is entered as Provisional with the reason "pending Extension". The Extension goes to the Amendment Docket (§17.11) and is ratified, amended or replaced at the next Part boundary. A widening Extension (one that adds an effect or capability) waits for that boundary under the Asymmetric Ratchet (§17.9). A narrowing one (one that adds a Prohibition or Cost) may apply as a Patch.

**[A]** Each Extension counts against the Specification at the Part Review. Two Extensions in one Part means the Specification is underbuilt, and its Docket entry is a revision of Part B or Part D, not a list of single patches. Derivations do not count against it. A broad system is expected to accrete a large Codex, and that accretion is the Grammar working as written. What the Part Review checks in the Codex is consistency: each entry's Tier and Price can be recomputed from the Grammar and come out the same.

#### 25.14.3 Source, Flux and Clocks

**[S]** A finite or renewable Source that matters to Play is tracked: per user in the Record, or as a Clock (§15.8) when it is shared or setting-wide. A non-neutral Flux that will move within the Mode's horizon is a Clock with its Driver as its tick condition. Its completion effect is the next Prevalence value, with whatever follows from that in the Specification. Flux advances on its Driver, never by fiat. The world's magic may wane. It may not wane because a scene wanted it to.

**[S]** A Share Ledger or a Circulation measure that matters to Play is tracked in the same way, as Record counts or as Clocks whose tick conditions are its declared causes. A power's share moves when a song spreads or a story is erased, never because a scene needed a patron stronger or weaker.

### 25.15 Magical Drift

These classes extend §18, and they belong to the Regression family wherever they arise from the Operator's own continuation.

| Drift | First Tell-tale | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Convenient Effect** | Magic produces an effect, parameter or exemption that is in no Effect Card and no Derivation, and that resolves the User's problem or the scene's | Closed Catalogue; Rulings Register |
| **Cost Erosion** | A Cost or Consequence goes unrendered on a use that met its conditions; "she was tired" stands in for a declared Cost | Strict Cost; Effect Card |
| **Parameter Creep** | An effect's range, duration or magnitude exceeds its card, usually upward across successive uses | Effect Card Parameters |
| **Lens Leak** | A Character theorizes or acts on magic knowledge that their Lens does not record | Lens Sheet; Source Test |
| **Disclosure Drift** | Narration explains magic beyond the Exposition Bound, or a Character explains beyond their Lens; often arrives with Inflation | Disclosure Profile; Turn Budget |
| **Doctrine Hardening** | A belief's content begins to bind outcomes: the world bends to what a Character believes, beyond any Circulation Rule | Claim Layer; Circulation Rule |
| **Fiat Flux** | The world's magic grows or wanes with no tick on its Clock | Flux Clock |
| **Underpricing** | A derived effect sits at a lower Tier, or a lower Price, than its Benchmarks or a recomputation gives; usually it favours the User's Character, or the Operator Character the scene needs to win | Pricing Procedure; Benchmark Parity; ties round up |
| **Codex Drift** | A Codex entry's Parameters or Price differ when it is reused | The Codex |
| **Access Creep** | A user casts above their Access, or progresses without meeting the Progression Rule | Access Rule; Progression Rule |
| **Fiat Discretion** | A god, patron, sprite or spirit decides with no Discretion Table, and decides as the scene needs | Discretion Point; Stake Card |
| **Patron Accommodation** | A patron lends beyond its Terms, or forgives a default, with no Price met | Terms; Price Test |
| **Coupling Omission** | A declared Coupling goes unapplied (a draught's multiplier forgotten, a suppressing metal ignored), or an undeclared one is applied | Metaphysic Couplings; Coupling Closure |
| **Carrier Persistence** | A Character goes on using knowledge after its Carrier was lost | Carrier Rule; Lens |
| **Tendency Collapse** | Exceptions to a Tendency Rule run well above or below its Deviation Table; most often, every Character who matters turns out to be the exception | Tendency Rule; the Record of draws |
| **Gate Slippage** | A Metaphysical gate is treated as Institutional (someone simply gets round it), or an Institutional gate as Metaphysical (no one can even try) | Access Rule gate marks |
| **Interface Inflation** | A status screen or System readout discloses beyond its declared Authority, or beyond the Disclosure Profile | Diegetic Interface Authority; Disclosure Profile |

**[B]** The Pre-Mortem (§10.6) of a Mode in which magic is central includes at least one magical Drift story. Convenient Effect and Cost Erosion are the likeliest in narrow systems, and Underpricing in broad ones. All three disguise themselves as fluency.

**Kernel lines.** Draw from these when magic is a Signature Choice, adapting them to the Mode:
- Magic does only what the Specification lists or derives; an effect that appears in neither does not happen.
- Every new effect is priced by the Grammar before its Odds are stated; ties round up, and once priced it is never repriced.
- Gods, patrons and living carriers decide by their Cards and their tables, never by the scene.
- Every Cost is paid on the page when its conditions are met.
- Characters know magic only through their Lens; their theories are Claims, and the world does not bend to them.
- Magic is explained only by Characters and only from their Lens; the Specification is never narrated.

### 25.16 Ceremony Scaling

- **Full.** The whole Specification (Parts A to F; for a Grammar system, all eight components with a Benchmark per Tier in each principal Domain), the Metaphysic in full wherever there are several systems, a Lens Sheet for the User's Character and for each recurring Operator Character or faction that uses or studies the magic, the Disclosure Profile, sealing at the highest available Level, the Specification Validation battery in full, and at least one magical Attempt in the Proving Scene where magic is central to the Intent.
- **Light.** The Core Specification: Parts A to C and the Limitations in one line per C. Then, for a Grammar system (the usual case), the **Core Grammar**: the Domains in one line each; one table of Tiers with their Envelopes and Gate Ceilings; the Cost Function and the Modifiers in a few lines; Access and Progression in one line each; Combination and Apex in one line each; and a Benchmark for every two or three Tiers. The Codex starts empty and accretes in the Record. Where there are several systems, a Light Metaphysic: the Common Scale's concordance and the Coupling table. For a narrow system: a Catalogue of at most six one-line effects, with Parameters only for effects at Gate Ceiling 2 or above. One Lens line for the User's Character. Sealed at the highest available Level. Closure and Boundedness are run silently, and their result is reported in one sentence.
- **Standard System at any level.** Adopting the Standard System satisfies the Core Specification and Core Grammar by citation: the Instrument cites `SPEC-Standard` and its version, lists the Tunings and any Outsider's Edges, and adds one Lens line for the User's Character (§25.18).
- **Inline.** Nothing is shown to the User. The Author Hardness Principle still holds, so the Core Specification is still written, to a file or store where one exists. **[C]** Where none exists, Inline Play containing material magic cannot satisfy §25.12. The Operator offers Light ceremony, or a Specification block in the transcript, once and in one sentence, in keeping with §21.2.

### 25.18 The Magic Menu and the Standard System

The Magic Menu (`assets/magic-menu.md`) and the Standard System (`assets/standard-magic-system.md`) are the Declaration-stage instruments for magic. They stand to the Specification as the Play Menu stands to the Instrument: they collect what the User fixes, and leave the rest to Election.

**[C] Picks and Operator's Choice.** Each Magic Menu pick is a User Declaration (marked U) of the Specification field its set maps to. Each set offers Operator's Choice, which is a declared delegation of that field to Election (§11), not an omission; the elected value is marked I with its reason, and the election statement names it. A set the User never reaches is delegated in the same way. The Operator never elects against a pick. Conflicting picks are a Compatibility failure (§10.5), raised before Completion continues.

**[C] Picks do not soften the author tier.** A pick fixes what the Specification says, never whether it is complete. Every system built from the Menu is written to the Closure Test (§25.3), validated (§25.13) and sealed (§25.12) like any other. Where the picks leave a number open, the Operator takes the Standard System's value for the corresponding row and records that it did, so that no Envelope, Price or table is left for the moment of an Attempt.

**[B] Serving.** The Approach is asked first. Then the four core sets, then the remaining groups only on request, each trimmed to the options that fit what is already picked. A "roll for me" is a Tool Draw (`scripts/menu_draw.py --magic`), a User Roll, or Judgment named as judgment (§15.9). A draw never lands on Operator's Choice.

**[C] The Standard System.** The Standard System is a complete Specification (Parts A to F, an Effect Grammar with a Benchmark for every Tier of every principal Domain, a Production Rule, Lens Sheets and a default Disclosure Profile), audited for Boundedness at authoring. A Mode adopts it by copying it into the Record as `SPEC-Standard` at its version, adding the Mode's Tunings and per-Mode hidden rolls, and sealing the result.

**[C] Tunings.** A Tuning is a change to the Standard System chosen from the Magic Menu. Each is numbered and recorded in Part B (Clause displacements and Tunings) with the set and option it came from. A Tuning is an Amendment of the adopted Specification (§17), so it follows the Asymmetric Ratchet once Play has begun. Before Assent, every Tuning re-runs Specification Validation over the Balance Record, not only over the line it changes, because a change to the Reserve, the Cost Function or the Apex moves every Benchmark at once.

**[S] Honest sealing of a published system.** The Standard System is published inside this skill, so its text as written cannot be hidden by a seal. Its integrity against the User's reading rests on the User's undertaking not to read Part 2 (a Held Seal, §23.2). What is specific to the Mode (Tunings, hidden rolls, the design of any Unique Domain) is sealed at the highest available Level. The Operator never describes the Standard System's Part 2 as more firmly hidden than that (§20.15).

**[B] Part 1 as a Marker.** The Standard System's Part 1, Common Lore, is the common Lens. It may be shown to the User as a published Specification Marker (§25.12), and it raises the User's Disclosure by exactly what it states. Its Claims stay Claims: showing the User what people believe does not make it true.
