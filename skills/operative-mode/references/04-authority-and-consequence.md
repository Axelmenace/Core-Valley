# Operative Mode Framework, Draft 0.4: §6 to §7

**Contains:** Authority Grants, the Standard Operation Set, the Residual Authority Clause, the Consequence Gate, the Telegraph Rule

**Read when:** Building the Authority Matrix; a Party's right to act is questioned; a Tier 2 or 3 consequence is in view.

---

## §6 Authority Architecture

### 6.1 Authority Grant

Authority is not represented by a single status. Every Authority Grant specifies five independent dimensions, set out in §6.2 to §6.6.

### 6.2 to 6.6 The five dimensions

- **Allocation (6.2).** User; Operator; either Party independently; both Parties jointly; a declared procedure; an imported external authority; no holder.
- **Exercise Rule (6.3).** Unilateral; consultation required; assent required; subject to veto; conditional upon a trigger; procedurally determined; alternating; reactive only.
- **Availability (6.4).** Permitted; restricted; suspended; prohibited.
- **Delegation (6.5).** Unavailable; revocably permitted; conditionally permitted; presently active; limited to specified operations. Delegation is an operation upon an existing Grant, and not an authority status.
- **Provenance (6.6).** The framework; external constraint; ratified Mode; User declaration; Operator election; joint assent; imported rule system; delegation; the Rulings Register.

### 6.7 Standard Operation Set

**[C]** Every Mode resolves, explicitly or through the Residual Authority Clause, the twelve Standard Operations.

| Code | Standard Operation |
| --- | --- |
| S1 | The User's Character: voluntary action and speech |
| S2 | The User's Character: interiority, memory, self-disclosed history |
| S3 | The User's Character: involuntary states (perception, injury, compulsion) |
| S4 | Operator Characters: action, speech, interiority |
| S5 | World facts: environment, institutions, history |
| S6 | Shared history and prior relationships between the Parties' Characters |
| S7 | The outcome of Attempts |
| S8 | Complications and pressure |
| S9 | Scene framing and the passage of time |
| S10 | Revelation of hidden state |
| S11 | Revision, retcon and annulment of Established Content |
| S12 | Record maintenance and Checkpoints |

### 6.8 Forms of authority

Diegetic agency (what an entity may attempt), Portrayal authority (who renders it), World authority (who establishes systemic fact), Outcome authority (who or what determines consequence), Narrative authority (who frames, directs attention and controls revelation), and Revision authority (who alters, reinterprets, supersedes or annuls). These six do not travel together unless the Mode says they do.

### 6.9 Authority Closure

**[C]** Every operation the intended Play foreseeably requires possesses a resolver: an explicit allocation, the Residual Authority Clause, a declared procedure, or an explicit prohibition.

Because of the fact that the Residual Authority Clause resolves every operation it is not displaced from, Authority Closure is satisfied by construction. The Validation test of Authority Closure (§10.5) is therefore no longer "does every operation have a resolver", which cannot be verified, but "does any operation resolved by the Residual Authority Clause need a different resolver for this Purpose", which can.

### 6.10 Residual Authority Clause

**[C]** Every operation not otherwise allocated by the Instrument is resolved as follows.

1. **S1, S2:** the User, unilaterally.
2. **S3:** the World Seat holder, through the Resolution Protocol, gated by the Consequence Gate (§7.6).
3. **S4:** the Operator, unilaterally, within each Character's own knowledge (§16.9).
4. **S5:** incidental and local facts by the World Seat holder (in its absence, by either Party, subject to Challenge); material facts by Proposal and uptake.
5. **S6:** jointly, by Proposal and uptake.
6. **S7:** the Resolution Protocol, administered by the Adjudicator Seat holder.
7. **S8:** the Operator, within the declared Pressure (§8.2).
8. **S9:** the Scene-Framer Seat holder; in its absence the Party whose Move closes a scene proposes the cut, and the other Party may redirect.
9. **S10:** by the Commitment Level and revelation condition of the hidden entry (§23).
10. **S11:** jointly, and between Parts only; within a Part, Corrections alone.
11. **S12:** the Continuity Seat holder; in its absence the Operator, with Checkpoints confirmed by the User.
12. **Any operation above Tier 1 of the Consequence Gate** not otherwise allocated: the Resolution Protocol with the Gate's conditions, or joint assent.
13. **Any operation upon the Mode itself:** Amendment (§17).

The Residual Authority Clause is what the framework does in the Instrument's silence (like in Earth IRL a constitution that reserves to the states or to the people every power not delegated, or a will whose residuary clause disposes of everything not specifically bequeathed).

### 6.11 Authority Matrix

The Instrument records only departures from the Residual Authority Clause, as rows of an Authority Matrix: Operation, Holder, Exercise, Note. Holder codes are **U**, **O**, **J** (Proposal and uptake), **R** (Resolution Protocol) **and N (no holder: prohibited).**

## §7 Consequence Architecture

### 7.1 to 7.5 The four axes

Consequences are classified along four independent axes.

- **Magnitude (7.2).** Incidental; material; major; identity-altering; systemic; Play-terminal.
- **Reversibility (7.3).** Automatically reversible; readily reversible; reversible at cost; conditionally reversible; irreversible within the continuity.
- **Duration (7.4).** Momentary; scene-bound; Part-bound; indefinite; permanent within the continuity.
- **Breadth (7.5).** Individual; relational; local; institutional; societal; setting-wide.

### 7.6 The Consequence Gate

**[C]** Every consequence falls into one of four Tiers, and its Tier is set by its highest value on any axis. That is, a consequence that is incidental in magnitude and irreversible in reversibility is a Tier 2 consequence, because irreversibility is a Tier 2 value.

| Tier | Values that place a consequence here | Settled by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Incidental | Incidental; automatically or readily reversible; momentary or scene-bound; individual | The Party holding the operation, unilaterally |
| 1 Material | Material; reversible at cost; Part-bound; relational or local | The Resolution Protocol |
| 2 Major | Major or identity-altering; conditionally reversible or irreversible; indefinite or permanent; institutional or wider | The Resolution Protocol, with pre-committed Stakes (§15.5), and the Telegraph Rule where the object is User-governed |
| 3 Terminal | Systemic or Play-terminal | Admissible only where the Instrument declares it admissible in advance; otherwise joint assent |

### 7.7 The Telegraph Rule

**[B, Default]** A Tier 2 or Tier 3 consequence upon a User-governed object is admissible only where it was preceded by a Telegraph, or where it follows from a User Attempt whose declared Stakes included it.

A Telegraph is a threat rendered within the fiction, perceptible to the User's Character, and followed by at least one opportunity for the User's Character to act upon it. The Telegraph Rule may be waived only by a User-set Delta, and a Mode that waives it says so in its Kernel.

This generalizes the Fairness Floor that The Drowned Throne v1.1 elected for itself, and it is imported from the practice of the soft move before the hard move (like in Earth IRL the wind-up an enemy shows before a heavy blow in a well-made game, or the warning shot, or the formal declaration that precedes a lawful war).

The world may be lethal. It may not be sudden.

### 7.8 Authority interaction

Authority narrows as the Tier rises. A Party may hold unilateral authority at Tier 0 and no authority at all at Tier 2 upon the same Content Object. The Instrument records such narrowing in the Authority Matrix (§6.11).
