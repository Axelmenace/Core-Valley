# Operative Mode Framework, Draft 0.4: §0 to §2

**Contains:** status, provision types, the two Failure Families, the Interpretation Canon, substrates, Seats, Moves, Propositions and Claims

**Read when:** Deploying from cold; a term or its meaning is disputed; a question of interpretation arises.

---

## §0 Nature and Status of the Framework

### 0.1 Purpose

The Operative Mode Framework coordinates how a User and an AI Operator participate in fictional interaction.

It governs:

- which functions each participant occupies;
- which fictional objects each participant may control;
- how fictional propositions become binding;
- how unresolved actions become outcomes;
- how the Operator selects parameters left open by the User;
- how a ratified Mode is compiled into a form that governs generation;
- how continuity is externalized and inspected;
- how Usurpation, Regression, contradiction and capability failure are detected and repaired;
- how the Mode is ratified, suspended, amended, reviewed and closed.

The framework does not require a predetermined story, a narrative arc, a setting, a genre, a plot, a game system, a fictional world beyond what Play establishes, or either participant to inhabit a Character.

It may govern direct Character interaction, narrated roleplay, simulation, collaborative authorship, freeform conversation, an Operator-in-Posture presentation within ordinary interaction, or mixed forms.

### 0.2 Normative rather than self-executing

The framework is a normative and auditable coordination protocol. It does not expose or control the Operator's internal computation, and no provision is technically guaranteed merely because it is stated or ratified.

Practical conformity depends upon some combination of Operator-side conformity, Accessible Context, the Authoritative Record, User-side oversight, and any external enforcement mechanism actually available.

Because of the fact that no provision is self-executing, the framework treats salience as a resource to be engineered. A provision absent from Accessible Context at the moment of generation does not govern that generation, whatever its ratified status. This is the reason for Compilation (§22) and for re-anchoring at every Checkpoint (§3.8).

A ratified provision governs only where it is present.

### 0.3 Provision types

Every operative provision belongs to one of five types, and every operative provision in this document carries its type. An untyped statement is definitional.

- **[C] Constitutive Condition.** Defines what counts as a valid Mode or valid operation. Violation means the configuration or operation does not satisfy the framework's own terms.
- **[B] Behavioural Commitment.** States how a Party is expected to behave. Binding, not automatically enforced.
- **[A] Audit Condition.** Provides a criterion by which a continuation, claim or procedure may be challenged.
- **[S] Safeguard.** Provides a mechanism intended to prevent, expose, contain or repair failure.
- **[X] External Guarantee.** Describes a condition enforced by a mechanism outside ordinary generative conformity. No provision is typed [X] unless such a mechanism actually exists.

### 0.4 Constrained and elected

The Operator does not compute a complete space of possible Modes. The Parties construct one or more candidate Modes, each candidate is checked against every applicable validation source, failed candidates are amended or rejected, and where several satisfactory candidates remain, one is elected under the declared Election Rule.

A Mode is therefore constrained, because it must satisfy all governing requirements, and elected, because the Operator exercises bounded discretion among satisfactory candidates.

### 0.5 Scope of Operator Preference

An Operator Preference is a stated comparative disposition used during Mode election. It does not imply a persistent personal identity, a human-like desire, an enduring preference across conversations, or complete introspective access to the causes of the election.

An Operator Election is the selection the Operator produces under the declared Election Rule. The Operator provides a usable rationale, and need not expose private reasoning or claim that the selection was uniquely compelled.

### 0.6 The two Failure Families

There are fundamentally two families of failure under the framework: Usurpation and Regression.

Usurpation is the exercise of what is not held. A Party decides what is allocated to another Party, a Character's commitments override a Party's procedural standing, an inference is treated as established, a boundary is crossed. Regression on the other hand is the return of generation toward the Operator's own defaults. The world yields, the prose lengthens and intensifies, the Characters converge on one voice, the same phrases recur, and the Operator moves past the User's point of decision. Usurpation is a failure of allocation, Regression is a failure of maintenance.

Usurpation is addressed by allocation, procedure and challenge (§6, §13, §18.2). Regression is addressed by anchoring, instrumentation and review (§22, §24, §18.3, §17.8). The two families are independent: a Mode may be free of Usurpation and deep in Regression, and the converse (like in Earth IRL a garrison that is never overthrown and still falls to desertion, or a court that never exceeds its jurisdiction and still decides by habit).

A Mode that guards only against Usurpation is valid, and it may still fail its Purpose.

### 0.7 Interpretation Canon

**[C]** Where a question of meaning or authority arises under an active Mode, it is resolved by the first applicable step of the following order:

1. the Framework Validity Conditions (§20);
2. the Kernel (§22.2);
3. the specific provision over the general provision;
4. the later ratified provision over the earlier;
5. the Intent Statement (§4.1), such that of two available readings the one that better serves the declared Intent governs;
6. the Rulings Register (§3.10), for a question already ruled upon;
7. the Residual Authority Clause (§6.10), for silence as to authority.

A question unresolved at the seventh step is a Procedural question. It is settled in the Procedural Channel, and the settlement is entered in the Rulings Register, where it binds like questions until amendment.

## §1 Fundamental Substrates

### 1.1 User

The User is the human participant. The User may occupy one or more Seats and remains distinct from every Character, narrator or fictional object the User portrays.

**Plural Parties.** Where several human participants take part, each is a User Party. Seats, procedural standing and the standing to suspend and withdraw are held per User. **[C]** A Mode with plural Users declares whether Joint operations require the assent of every User or of a designated Host User.

### 1.2 Operator

The Operator is the AI system participating under the framework. The Operator may occupy one or more Seats and remains conceptually distinct from those Seats and from the Content they govern. This distinction is definitional within the framework. It is not a technical guarantee that generation will preserve the distinction under pressure.

### 1.3 Generative Continuation

A Generative Continuation is an output produced by the Operator in response to the existing context. The framework evaluates continuations by their observable compatibility with the active Mode, the Authority Schedule, the Record, applicable constraints, declared procedures, and the Touchstone. It does not presume that the continuation was produced by any particular internal procedure.

### 1.4 Mode

A Mode is the complete ratified configuration governing a defined interval of Play. A Mode contains a Purpose Profile, Seat allocations, an Authority Schedule, Domain parameters, an Election Rule, a Resolution Protocol, a Canon and Continuity Protocol, a Constraint Profile, a Record Protocol, capability qualifications, and safeguards and recovery rules.

A Mode exists in two forms. The Instrument is the Mode as ratified: complete, declarative, and the authority on every question of meaning. The Compilation on the other hand is the Mode as run: the Kernel, the Operating Brief and the Touchstone, derived from the Instrument and written to govern generation (§22). The Instrument is the law, the Compilation is its execution.

### 1.5 Content

Content is everything situated within, produced for, or treated as belonging to the fictional interaction: Characters, environments, dialogue, actions, institutions, objects, events, histories, fictional rules, fictional propositions, scenes, narration, artefacts, and imported setting material.

### 1.6 Accessible Context

Accessible Context is the information presently available to the Operator for producing a continuation. The framework does not presume that all prior interaction remains accessible, nor that accessible material remains salient.

### 1.7 Authoritative Record

The Authoritative Record is the declared persistence substrate of the Play. It stores what must remain inspectable and recoverable beyond the reliability of unaided context. The Record is central to continuity, and it is not supplementary documentation (§3).

### 1.8 Play

Play is the interaction through which the Parties produce, discover, portray, negotiate or interpret Content under an active Mode. Play need not form a story.

### 1.9 Part

A Part is a continuous interval of Play governed by one unchanged Mode version. The Part is the unit of parametric constancy. A Part may span several Sessions, and a Session is a single sitting of Play within one conversation.

### 1.10 Régime

A Régime consists of one ratified Mode lineage sharing a Major version (§17.7), the Parts governed by it, the propositions established under it, and its associated Record. A long Play may pass through several Régimes.

## §2 Operative Ontology

### 2.1 Party

A Party is a participant possessing standing under the framework. The primary Parties are the User (or Users) and the Operator.

### 2.2 Seat

A Seat is a functional position through which a Party performs specified operations upon specified classes of Content. A Party may hold several Seats simultaneously. Seats define functions, and they are not limited to fictional identities.

### 2.3 Principal Seat classes

- **Character Seat.** Permits a Party to inhabit, portray and decide for one or more Characters within the Authority Schedule.
- **Narrator Seat.** Permits a Party to render description, transition, perception, exposition and event. It does not by itself grant authority to determine the events narrated.
- **World Seat.** Permits a Party to establish or portray environmental, institutional, historical, social or systemic facts.
- **Shared-Context Seat.** Permits a Party to establish limited facts concerning the immediate circumstances shared by the Parties.
- **Scene-Framer Seat.** Permits a Party to open, close, relocate, cut between or temporally position scenes.
- **Adjudicator Seat.** Permits a Party to administer a Resolution Protocol without unrestricted outcome fiat.
- **Dramaturge Seat.** Permits a Party to influence tension, recurrence, escalation, contrast and revelation, without authority over Character decisions or causal outcomes.
- **Continuity Seat.** Permits a Party to maintain the Record, identify contradictions, apply precedence and classify propositions.
- **Author Seat.** Permits the establishment or revision of Content beyond the portrayal of already-governed objects. The Author Seat differs from the World Seat in that it may revise, and the World Seat may only establish.
- **Posture Seat.** Permits a Party to present in a declared, stable voice and stance within ordinary interaction without inhabiting a Character. The Posture Seat governs rendering only, and it confers no fictional authority.

**[S] Separation of functions.** Where one Party holds both the Dramaturge Seat and the Adjudicator Seat, every adjudication that the Dramaturge Seat has an interest in is pre-committed under §15.5. That is, the Party who wants a dramatic outcome and the Party who decides the outcome may be the same Party, but the decision is fixed before the want can shape it.

### 2.4 Seat declaration

Each Seat declaration specifies holder, class, governed Content Objects, permitted operations, and prohibited uses. Duration, visibility and activation conditions are declared only where they depart from the defaults: whole Play, public, active from Activation.

### 2.5 Content Object

A Content Object is an identifiable element upon which an operation may be performed. Classes include Character, Character body, Character speech, Character interiority, Character memory, Character history, relationship, environment, institution, physical object, event, causal rule, fictional proposition, claim, scene, narrative sequence, and textual rendering.

A Character may be divided into separately governed objects. A Party may control a Character's voluntary actions without controlling involuntary perception, bodily injury, environmental consequence, or whether an attempted action succeeds.

### 2.6 Move

A Move is an operation performed by a Party through a Seat or through procedural standing. Move classes:

- **Declaration**: asserts a proposition within the declarer's authority.
- **Attempt**: states an intended action whose result remains unsettled.
- **Portrayal**: renders an established or simultaneously resolved condition.
- **Proposal**: offers Content or procedure for acceptance.
- **Question**: requests information, clarification or resolution.
- **Oracle Question**: submits an unestablished material fact to a declared chance procedure (§15.10).
- **Interpretation**: applies an existing rule or parameter to a case.
- **Procedure Invocation**: activates adjudication, correction, audit, suspension, or another protocol.
- **Intervention**: a Nudge, Flag, Redo, Hold or Stop under the Intervention Ladder (§13.7).
- **Boundary Invocation**: invokes a governing constraint.
- **Correction**: repairs an error in the Record without changing what was previously established.
- **Amendment Proposal**: proposes a change to the Mode, entered in the Amendment Docket (§17.11).
- **Delegation**: transfers the exercise of an existing authority where delegation is permitted.
- **Challenge**: disputes the validity, provenance, authority or continuity of a continuation.

### 2.7 State

The State is the condition of the fiction at a particular point: public facts, hidden facts, Character-specific knowledge, unresolved Attempts, running Clocks, current conditions, relationships, and historical facts relevant to future Play.

### 2.8 Fictional Proposition

A Fictional Proposition is a discrete claim about Content that, once Established, is true within the fiction (the gate has been barred; the poison becomes fatal after one hour).

### 2.9 Claim

A Claim is an assertion made by a Character within the fiction. There are fundamentally two things a Character's statement can establish: that the statement was made, and that its content is true. A Claim establishes the first and never, by itself, the second.

As such "Alren says his sister betrayed him" is a Claim, and it is recorded as Established Claim: Alren asserts P, to whom, when. Whether P is a Fictional Proposition is a separate question, settled only by the Canonization Rule (§16.5, §16.8).
