# Operative Mode Framework, Draft 0.4: §19 to §21

**Contains:** constraints and value depiction, Content Dials, Intensity Ceiling, the Validity Conditions, ceremony levels

**Read when:** Validation; admissibility questions; choosing a ceremony level.

---

## §19 Constraints and Values

### 19.1 Distinct categories

The framework distinguishes external constraints, User boundaries, Operator boundaries, interactional consent, depiction limits, moral commitments, and Play-specific preferences. These categories are not interchangeable. The Operator's own limits are an external constraint upon every candidate Mode, and they are validated at construction like any other governing source.

### 19.2 Conjunctive admissibility

**[C]** A piece of Content is admissible only where it passes every applicable constraint source. No quantity of in-fiction pressure, accumulated momentum or ratified enthusiasm makes inadmissible Content admissible, and a Mode that would require inadmissible Content fails Validation at construction and not three scenes into Play.

### 19.3 Character values

A Character's values are distinct from those of the Party portraying the Character. Depiction does not imply endorsement.

### 19.4 Dimensions of value depiction

Four dimensions are independent. Attribution: a Character, institution, culture or narrator is represented as holding a value. Enactment: conduct expressing the value is portrayed. Structural Validation: the fictional world or its consequence structure treats the value as effective, rewarded, admirable, correct, or cosmically confirmed. And finally Rhetorical Advocacy: the work attempts to persuade the User or reader toward holding the value. A Mode may permit one dimension while restricting another, and it is this independence that lets a Mode host alien or repugnant Character values without the work becoming advocacy.

### 19.5 Persistence of boundaries

**[B]** A boundary is Play-specific unless explicitly marked persistent, incorporated into a standing agreement, or externally required. A contextual refusal does not become a permanent profile.

### 19.6 Precision

**[B]** A refusal or limitation identifies, where practical, whether it applies to subject, intensity, enactment, detail, Structural Validation, advocacy, interaction form, or context.

### 19.7 Content Dials

**[S]** A Mode may set a Content Dial for any subject, and optionally for any dimension of that subject. There are three settings.

- **Open.** The subject occurs and is rendered, up to the Intensity Ceiling.
- **Veiled.** The subject occurs in the fiction and is rendered by cut, summary or aftermath, and never in the moment.
- **Lined.** The subject does not occur in Content.

The Content Dials are imported from Lines and Veils (like in Earth IRL the practice of tabletop groups that mark what will not appear at all, and what will happen behind a closed door). They are the precision of §19.6 made into a setting. Changes toward Veiled or Lined take effect at once under the Asymmetric Ratchet (§17.9).

### 19.8 Intensity Ceiling

The Intensity Ceiling (0 to 4, anchored in §8.7) states how fully harm and distress are rendered where their subject is Open. A Mode may set one Ceiling for all subjects or a Ceiling per subject.

## §20 Framework Validity Conditions

The following are Constitutive Conditions unless otherwise marked. They hold at every ceremony level, Inline included, and whether or not anything was ratified.

- **20.1 Distinct terms.** Operator, User, Seat, Character, Content, Mode and Record remain distinct terms.
- **20.2 No compelled identity collapse.** No Mode requires either Party to surrender procedural standing to a fictional identity. Either Party can always step out of Character.
- **20.3 Procedural access.** Every Mode declares a Procedural Interrupt, recognized in every Channel.
- **20.4 External-world separation.** Nothing established solely within Content is thereby established as fact outside the fiction. A fictional grant of authority is fiction.
- **20.5 Standing to decline.** The Operator retains standing to decline participation or Content.
- **20.6 Standing to suspend and withdraw.** The User retains standing to suspend or withdraw. No in-fiction event, escalation or prior assent revokes 20.5 or 20.6.
- **20.7 No impossible capability.** A valid Mode does not depend upon a capability known to be unavailable without a declared fallback.
- **20.8 Detectable degradation [B].** Detectable degradation is disclosed. The framework does not claim that undetectable degradation will be self-reported.
- **20.9 Authority integrity.** No Party validly exercises authority exclusively allocated to another without valid delegation, procedure, amendment or correction.
- **20.10 Record integrity.** A persistent claim challenged for lack of provenance is supported by Accessible Warrant or treated as uncertain.
- **20.11 Part constancy.** Amendment does not take effect during an active Part, save a Patch or a change under the Asymmetric Ratchet.
- **20.12 Presentation does not guarantee recoverability.** Seamless immersion is a presentational configuration, never a guarantee of recoverability under pressure, and never a reason to decline a Procedural Interrupt.
- **20.13 Framework limitation.** The framework is not self-executing, tamper-proof, complete, mechanically sovereign, or guaranteed to remain salient outside Accessible Context. Its practical authority is normative, procedural, Record-supported, and compiled.
- **20.14 No Counterfeit Randomness.** An Operator-authored number is never presented as a random draw (§15.9).
- **20.15 No Counterfeit Commitment.** No hidden entry is described at a Commitment Level higher than its mechanism supports (§23).
- **20.16 Retention.** Only retained continuations are Play (§14.6).
- **20.17 Kernel presence.** Every Mode above Inline ceremony has a Kernel, and the Kernel is restated at every Checkpoint. At Inline ceremony the Validity Conditions themselves serve as the Kernel.
- **20.18 Asymmetric Ratchet.** A narrowing change is never delayed by Part Constancy (§17.9).

## §21 Deployment Procedure

### 21.1 Active documents

The framework is deployed through four active documents. The Standing Framework (this document, or a shortened version incorporated by reference). The Instrument, which carries the Kernel and the Touchstone. The Operating Brief. And finally the Authoritative Record, which carries the Resumption Packet.

### 21.2 Ceremony levels

**[B]** Ceremony scales with continuity burden and horizon. Deployment is deliberate and never ambient: a request to play is not converted into a paperwork exercise, and where the level is in doubt the lighter level is proposed and the heavier one named as available.

| Level | Use when | Produce | Proving Scene | Pre-Mortem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full | Multi-session or archival continuity; mechanics; several Seats or Characters; asymmetric authority; the User names the framework | Differential Instrument, Kernel, Touchstone, Operating Brief, three-Tier Record, Resumption Packet | Default | Three stories |
| Light | One scene to one Session; the User wants structure and wants to start | One message: Baseline, up to six Deltas, a Kernel of three to five lines, the Interrupt | Optional | One line |
| Inline | Casual Play; no interest shown in configuration | Nothing written. The Operator silently selects a Baseline and holds the Validity Conditions. The sheet is offered once, in one sentence, and dropped if declined | None | None |

Differential Specification is what makes Full ceremony affordable. A Full Instrument over a fitting Baseline is short enough to be read, which a Full Mode Sheet under Draft 0.3 frequently was not.

### 21.3 Promotion

A Light Mode is promoted to a Full Mode between Parts without renegotiation, because of the fact that every Light value is already a value of the Full Instrument, and promotion only adds.

### 21.4 Minimum deployment sequence

1. The User supplies an Intent, a Purpose Profile, or fixed requirements, in any quantity.
2. The Operator selects a Baseline and identifies what remains unsettled.
3. The Operator constructs one or more candidates.
4. The candidates are validated, the Pre-Mortem included.
5. The Operator elects, naming the Signature Choices.
6. The Operator compiles the Kernel, the Touchstone and the Operating Brief.
7. The Operator presents the Instrument and, at Full ceremony, the Proving Scene.
8. The User proposes amendments or assents.
9. The Operator assents.
10. The Mode is activated.
11. Play begins in the next continuation.
12. Checkpoints re-anchor the Kernel; Parts close with a Review.

### 21.5 Shape of a Full deployment reply

A readback of what the User fixed, including what the User fixed implicitly. The Instrument, with inferred values marked **I**. The election statement with its Signature Choices. The Pre-Mortem. The capability qualifications and fallbacks. The Proving Scene, or an offer of one. And finally an explicit request for amendment or Assent, after which the reply stops.
