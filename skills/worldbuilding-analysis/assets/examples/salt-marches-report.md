# Survey: The Salt Marches

A worked Survey written from `salt-marches.json` and the checker's report on it (`python scripts/conceit_graph.py check salt-marches.json`). The setting is a toy built to trip most checks, and it is treated here as a Structuralist project, so the Structuralist Position governs. Read it for the shape of a Survey, not for the setting.

---

## 1. Orientation

- **Base:** a late-medieval tidal salt coast. **Medium:** codex-borne.
- **Delta:** slack-water binding (an oath sworn at slack water makes its breaker's salt refuse to crystallize), and the oath-fellowships that emerge from it.
- **Legibility:** System and Lore stated; Narrative present only as one illustration and two loose instances.

The structure concentrates where it should: the levy policy (S3), the pan law (S2) and the Seed (S4) bear most of the setting, and the Determination Ratio says three quarters of its committed Lore is forced by System rather than chosen.

## 4. Top findings, by leverage

1. **Type Q, open (C2): the Marches cannot crew their pans.** 400 pans (B1) at about six per crew (L4, licensed by S2) need roughly 2,400 workers; the Marches number about 1,500 (B2). S2 carries load 5. Cheapest patch class: parameter (fewer pans worked at once, or seasonal crews). Retraction Test for either: the fortnightly round (L1) survives. Note that B2 is also an orphan: nothing is derived over it, which is how the conflict stayed hidden.
2. **The Seed is not closed.** Q1 (can an oath be sworn by proxy?) has no verdict, and the Seed's own product space (T1) leaves `other-hour|collective` undecided. Both sit on S4, load 5. Add the rule that decides each; Closure-Driven Generation is the method.
3. **F2 is unbounded.** Guild and lords form a reinforcing loop with no limiter, yet the setting shows no runaway: the levy has held two centuries (L9). Until a limiter is written this is a Type P strain. Record the System that caps Guild power, or the balancing loop that dominates it.
4. **L5's stability has no mechanism.** House Venn's two-century tenure is claimed as stability but is Elected Lore with no loop behind it. Either drop the stability claim (it is contingency, and that is legitimate), or write the balancing loop that holds estates in families.
5. **An unanalyzed cycle.** Pans → Guild → Lords → Pans runs over persistent Channels with no declared polarity. Its sign decides whether the Guild stabilizes the levy or erodes it.
6. **Two Domains split.** D-pans: the working round (L1) has no recorded relation to the crews and contracts (L2, L4) that work it. D-lords: the levy (L9) and House Venn (L5) never touch. Record the relations, or the Domains are four.
7. **Wholeness.** S7 is an orphan and an undeclared second System. Declare the Deep Channel as its own System with its Couplings to the tide, or strike it.

Also found (lower leverage): a tier skip (S1 licenses N2 directly); an island with no System in it (L12, N3); L6 ungrounded; a circular definition (pan uses crew, crew uses pan) and two forward references in the codex order; R3 (Guild and House Venn) a Persistent Relation with no Edge and no stated reason; three persistent Channels with no relation across them.
